## Increment144: forward-only raid-chain streaks

`P9_STREAK_ENABLED=false` is the default. Enabling it does not migrate, replay or award historical completions. Coverage starts at the next new verified receipt processed under the gate. Existing records show unknown coverage, not invented zero historical wins. Only the atomic successful two-chat claim increments once, alongside the real holder, lineage, scores and completion notices. Each covered stop stores its count and coverage start; the recipient of a third-or-later covered win earns **Unbroken streak · 3 hand-offs**, separately from the older Chain of3 badge.

A lapse means a covered verified claim window ended without binding, or that verified hop was cancelled by current policy/capacity. The next trusted mutation records it before replacing the pending hop. Reads can logically project zero for an already-expired covered window but never write. No fixed inter-raid gap is imposed. API acceptance, unverified/unknown dispatch, source stream retirement alone, reconnect, hidden tabs and viewer absence do not break or increment a streak. Removing a mark does not rewrite anonymous past results; current policy cancellation of a pending verified hop does break it.

The bounded version1 tracker lives only in the existing registry transaction, with counts1–16, best, coverage time, active-hop hash, last completed-stop association and one closed-enum lapse. No actor identifiers, collection, index, worker, provider read or timer is added. Invalid/rewound tracking metadata fails closed to unknown; normal game authority remains unchanged. Gate-off projections keep their previous top-level contract. The feature is candidate code, not parent-verified or physically tested. Only the eventual parent-controlled read-only zero-traffic preview is authorized; production6401, cc preview and pinned J5/J6 remain unchanged.

## Increment145: local review rules and latest-mark veto

Operator-only `MARK_AUTO_APPROVE_ENABLED=true` enables review for **new** eligible marks. `MARK_REVIEW_BLOCKLIST` is a JSON array of at most32 valid word/emoji terms and at most4096UTF-8 bytes. Terms use the existing NFKC/lowercase/ß→ss normalization and match literal substrings of normalized marks; no regular expressions, AI or provider is involved. `[]` approves every otherwise-valid eligible new mark. An invalid boolean, JSON, term or bound keeps manual approval. This is a local review rule, not a universal safety guarantee.

Existing hard rejection (unsafe word/URL/control/invalid word-or-one-emoji/size limits) stays rejection, not a retained held item. Otherwise-valid custom matches remain pending and private until signed moderator approval. Clean new marks become approved in the same quota/cooldown/dedupe transaction. Old pending marks are never retroactively approved when configuration changes. The default remains manual. No browser parameter or submitted approved flag can supply trusted policy.

Signed same-channel broadcasters/moderators may use exact `!relic veto` or the compatible `!relic veto-last`. Both remove the latest retained mark using existing current/retained-session logic, whether approved or pending. Non-moderators, copied messages, bots and blocked actors cannot veto. Actual authorized removal purges carried/archive/search views and cancels still-queued completion/P7 proofs; merely sending an unauthorized veto cannot cancel jobs. Already-dispatched effects and downloaded files cannot be recalled. Forgetting, vetoing and blocking never refund the five-per-viewer stream quota or30-second cooldown.

All review and veto work is local candidate source pending parent QA; production6401 and cc preview are unchanged. No live chat/P7 provider action is performed by the author. Moderation adds no scopes, provider reads, migration, worker or timer. Clip work remains design-only for the separately authorized increment146.

## Increment146: gate corrections and unwired clip design

The expiry regression now explicitly asserts a new control POST is accepted before continuing the signed raid/claim. Its valid CSRF is obtained **before** expiry and reused by the same authenticated session at the exact deadline. The normal fixture helper's extra status GET at that same frozen millisecond otherwise performs the already-authorized retirement CAS, advances the watermark and makes the immediately following same-ms POST stale. This is a fixture sequence correction, not weaker production recovery or a delay workaround. The two new public-page browser gates retain HTTPS private fixture origins and expose only public HTTP pages on registered loopback5191; OAuth HTTPS rules are unchanged. Parent native results remain pending.

Clip code is **design/injected-mock only**, not imported by runtime, webhooks, controls, OAuth or public clients. `CLIPS_ENABLED=false` and `CLIPS_ALLOW_CREATE=false` are documented defaults, with a separate explicit activation-approval proof required by the design. Even setting these environment variables true cannot wire a production dispatcher. There is no production transport, ledger, endpoint, worker, confirmation loop, migration or startup action. No new consent URL or default scope is implemented.

The proposed creator is bot **raidrelicbot1591804828**, with a **USER** token and `clips:edit`; APP tokens cannot create clips. The destination Keeper is `broadcaster_id`, not the creator identity. A future explicit human bot-consent flow must preserve the existing3 baseline+4 optional P7 permissions and add clips:edit (8 total); the API's additional required scope is just clips:edit. No Keeper reconsent is required by that API, but per-Keeper feature permission, current consent, live matching stream, clips enabled and creator eligibility remain mandatory. Follower/subscriber/banned/timed-out restrictions still apply. Parent reported the official [Create Clip reference](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/reference/#create-clip) was HTTP200 at03:24; this author made no provider calls.

A future explicitly awaited dispatcher could trigger **once on a fresh verified raid receipt**, not Send Off acceptance, completion, an old hop, replay or GET. The pure plan caps creation at30seconds from that receipt and requires fresh bot identity/client/scope proof. An injected ledger must atomically reserve the exact domain-separated event/target key as dispatching **before** any network attempt. A second fresh authorization check follows reservation. Dispatching, accepted, blocked, unknown and crash-ambiguous keys are never re-created; timeout/401/403/404/429/5xx do not schedule retries or privilege escalation. There is no implemented production ledger or provider adapter; any future adapter must bound response bytes/time and never log tokens or raw bodies.

HTTP202 means **creation request accepted**, not a created or visible clip. A separate, explicit, at-most-one mock confirmation within60seconds uses Get Clips (USER or APP allowed), requires the returned clip ID and exact configured broadcaster, and discards edit_url, creator IDs, titles and thumbnails. Only the same **completed** retained stop plus fresh allowed-policy proof can produce `https://clips.twitch.tv/<validated-id>`. No public link or database attachment is currently wired. Wrong broadcaster, missing/pending result, failed binding, stale/deleted stop or withdrawn policy yields no attachment. There is no automatic polling, iframe, remote image, public arbitrary URL or paid task.

Timing is best effort: Create Clip captures roughly90seconds around its request and normally publishes the last30seconds, roughly through5seconds after request. It cannot guarantee the entire20-second OBS arrival; the broadcaster's video must itself include the overlay. Waiting up to180seconds for binding may miss arrival, which is why this design triggers at receipt and associates privately until completion. If binding fails, an external clip may already exist; local forget/opt-out cannot promise Twitch-side deletion or recall downloaded footage. This consent/privacy tradeoff requires owner approval **before** any future activation. No clip creation, consent, refresh, live test or deletion occurred here.
